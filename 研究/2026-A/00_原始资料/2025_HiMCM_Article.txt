Judging Process
Judging is conducted blindly without
identifying information about teams or
schools. As open-ended problems, 
submissions are evaluated on their 
assumptions, reasoning, and modeling
approach rather than adherence to a
single correct answer with a rubric. All
submissions are reviewed electronically,
allowing for a diverse and highly qualified
judging panel to easily review. Each
paper is initially evaluated by at least
two preliminary judges. We extend our
sincere thanks to these judges for their
thoughtful and thorough assessments.
After the preliminary round, papers are
categorized as Finalist, Meritorious, 
Honorable Mention, or Successful Partic-
ipant. Finalist papers advance to a final
round of judging, where the most excep-
tional submissions are selected as Out-
standing. This year, 105 papers advanced
to final judging, with eight ultimately
earning the Outstanding designation.

Sensitivity Analysis
Sensitivity analysis is an important com-
ponent of the modeling process, as it 
examines how changes in assumptions,
parameters, or input data affect the 
results of a model. It helps determine the
robustness, reliability, and overall 
usefulness of the model. Effective sensi-
tivity analysis often includes revisiting
simplifying assumptions and evaluating
how variations in key inputs influence
outcomes. While there is no single 
required method, judges look for clear
rationale behind the approach taken and
thoughtful interpretation of the results.
Strong papers use sensitivity analysis to
justify the level of confidence in their
model and its conclusions. While some
teams present this analysis as a dedicated
section, those that integrate it throughout
their work, connecting it directly to
model development and implementation
stand out. Judges feel that a well-ex-
ecuted sensitivity analysis demonstrates
a deeper understanding of the model’s
behavior and its practical implications.
Solutions to this year’s Problem A, Emer-

things as the walking speed of 
responders, changes due to the presence
of smoke or clutter, or the number
and/or condition of possible victims in
the building. In general, such explorations
involve systematic variation of parameter
values so that the influence of that 
parameter may be isolated. Ideally, such 
influences would then be compared and
interpreted in the context of the overall
objective of the emergency sweep model.

Citations, References, AI
Usage Report
Teams must give credit where credit is
due.  It is okay to use existing models
found elsewhere, but teams that do this
but do not cite, lose the trust of the
judges.  Teams that use existing models
must cite their source(s) within the paper
at the point they present the model and
include a reference citation in the back
of the paper.  This is also true for all graphs
and tables taken from literature.  All data,
figures, graphs, and tables that come from
outside sources require documentation at
the point in the paper that they appear.
Lack of documentation will result in a lower
designation.  Teams should use “in line”
documentation with footnotes or endnotes
to give proper credit to outside sources.  
With the increasing availability of AI tools,
COMAP recognizes that teams may use
these resources to support their modeling
process and the preparation of their 
submissions. Any use of AI must be 
treated as an external source and clearly 
documented in an AI Use Report. This 
documentation should provide transparency
into how the tool was used by including 
the excerpts of prompts and outputs.
Teams have used AI tools in a variety of
ways, such as gathering or summarizing
data, exploring coding approaches, or 
generating potential modeling strategies.
When used thoughtfully, these tools can
support and enhance the modeling 
process. However, reliance on AI without
careful evaluation can weaken a submission.
Like any resource, AI outputs should be
approached critically. Teams are responsible
for any AI-generated content they choose
to incorporate. Thoughtful and responsible
use of AI strengthens both the 
credibility and quality of a submission.
