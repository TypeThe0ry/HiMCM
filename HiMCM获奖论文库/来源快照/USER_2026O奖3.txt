Research on Comprehensive Environmental Assessment of Sport
Mega-Events
As public awareness of sustainability grows, mega sporting events are facing stricter scrutiny and public
pressure. Beyond excitement and huge economic gains, comprehensive evaluations for such events are in urgent
need in order to strive for greener, more responsible decision making. This is especially obvious when it comes
to the case study of Super Bowl LIX in New Orleans. Even though the NFL had taken measures to mitigate the
environmental footprint, Super Bowl LIX still left a significant impact on the local environment.
In this study, we primarily developed two sets of comprehensive evaluation metrics for the Super Bowl and the
World Cup, respectively.
Covering more than 14 contributing factors, our City Impact Evaluation Model (CIEM), which evaluates
potential host cities for Super Bowl, is established based on 5 core indices of environmental impact: Carbon Emis-
sion Index (CEI), Energy Consumption Index (ECI), Water Consumption Index (WCI), Air Quality Index (AQI),
and Landfill Index (LFI). Next, we determined the weights of each index during multi-criteria decision-making
(MCDM) by strategically combining the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and the Entropy Weight Method
(EWM). The AHP-EWM MCDM provides an adaptable, transparent, and structured framework for weighting,
ensuring objectivity within subjectivity while yielding a set of balanced, practical results. Finally, we utilized
Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) to generate a comprehensive scoring for
each potential host, recommending Inglewood, California, for future Super Bowl events, which has the highest
scoring.
To adapt CIEM to assess the eligibility of potential host countries for the World Cup, we established our
National Impact Evaluation Model (NIEM) . NIEM encompasses 6 core indices of environmental impact: CEI
(adapted), ECI (adapted), WCI (adapted), AQI (adapted), LFI (adapted), and a new Environmental-Industrial Index
(EII). Similarly, NIEM employs a smart combination of both AHP and Criteria Importance Through Intercriteria
Correlation (CRITIC) due to the interdependence between the 6 indices; the AHP-CRITIC MCDM guarantees
transparency and objectivity within subjectivity and effectively handles the interconnections of the data. Ulti-
mately, TOPSIS is again used to yield a composite score for each potential host country, selecting Germany with
the highest score as the best choice for hosting future World Cup events.
Notably, we conductedsensitivity analyses for CIEM by adjusting the AHP-to-EWM ratios and the numbers
of participants in a Super Bowl event. First, AHP-to-EWM ratios determine which weighting method is more
influential. By slightly fluctuating the ratio, our final scoring for the potential host cities remained approximately
unchanged, proving CIEM to be robust and reliable. Second, the number of participants is also altered, resulting
again in slight fluctuation in the results, further corroborating CIEM’s robustness and reliability.
Assessing CIEM and NIEM, both integrate a relatively wide range of contributing factors that constitute
5 and 6 indices, respectively, ensuring comprehensiveness. Moreover, both models are adaptable to a variety
of host cities or countries. CIEM can be applied to most US states, while NIEM can be used to evaluate most
countries across the world. Nonetheless, due to the lack of data, CIEM and NIEM are both approximation models
based on assumptions. If enhanced with data currently unavailable, CIEM and NIEM will be considerably more
accurate and practical.

1 Introduction
1.1 Background
As technology develops, an increasing amount of population is paying attention to the environmental footprint
and sustainability behind major sporting events, trying to make the world a better place for all life. Major sporting
events such as the Super Bowl and the World Cup generate great economic revenue, but along with great revenue,
there are great sacrifices to the environment due to logistics, waste and energy behind the scenes. Although such
events are made more sustainable every year, our team wants to make these events not only based on revenue,
but also sustainability of the city held as one of the core factors to consider in planning. With this thought, we’re
hired by Consulting Organization Making Athletics Planet positive (COMAP) , we created both the City Im-
pact Evaluation Model (CIEM) and National Impact Evaluation model (NIEM) that assess the environmental
footprint of the host cities and countries for the Super Bowl and the World Cup, respectively, striving for greener,
more responsible sporting events in the years to come.
1.2 Problem Restatement
In this study, we aim to complete the following tasks.
• Task 1: To analyze the environmental footprints of a specific event (Super Bowl LIX) and to identify factors
that impact the environmental footprint of this mega sporting event by referring to existing metrics.
• Task 2: To create a qualitative or quantitative model to determine where to hold the next Super Bowl
based solely on environmental concerns.
• Task 3: To apply our model in Task 2 to determine (1) which of the previously hosting cities—Inglewood
(CA), Glendale (AZ), Las Vegas (NV), and New Orleans (LA)—should host the Super Bowl again, and
(2) which of the three selected cities that have never hosted before represents the most sustainable and
environmentally friendly choice.
• Task 4: To adapt our model to assess the environmental footprint of a single-sport championship tour-
nament (the World Cup ) and to determine the best host location while accounting for the event’s unique
environmental challenges; to develop stratagems that minimize environmental impact during the event
(short term) and are economically and logistically practical (long term); to compare the key environmen-
tal factors between the Super Bowl and the single-sport championship tournament (the World Cup) and to
provide explanations.
• Task 5 To write a one-page persuasiveletter to the NFL committee recommending our chosen city for Super
Bowl LXIII in 2029, emphasizing the environmental advantages of the city and its alignment with the NFL ’s
sustainability goals.

2 Assumptions with Justification
Assumption 1: The scale and duration of the Super Bowl are constant in all cities and all times. We assume that
there are 80000 participants (including athletes and audiences) and a period of 3 days for simplicity[ 1].
Justification: This makes sure that our comparison between cities is fair and consistent and that the differences in
the model’s output are due to characteristics of the host city rather than variations in the event’s scale or duration.
To clarify, the number of audiences for one Super Bowl event is about 70000 people, according to our research.
Since every Super Bowl event has about the same number of audience, we simply set the maximum people in a
Super Bowl event as 80000 people due to other faculty members and participants.
Assumption 2: The behavior of Super Bowl participants is consistent in all cities and events.
Justification: This makes sure the effect of the city’s infrastructures and the calculations are not affected by mul-
tifarious personal situations.
Assumption 3: State-level data serves as proxies for city-level environmental characteristics.
Justification: While some variation exists within states, state-level data is considered to be comprehensive and
standardized, which can reflect the overall environmental characteristics of the host city.
Assumption 4: Environmental policies remain stable from 2020 to 2029, and all urban infrastructure systems
function normally without disruptions during the event.
Justification: This ensures that the model assesses cities based on their existing environmental governance and
typical infrastructures, rather than being influenced by unpredictable policy changes or infrastructure failures.

Assumption 5: The number of audiences for a World Cup event is about 3.5 × 106[5]. The number of audi-

Justification: These estimations are based on historical attendance data and therefore reflect a typical number of
participants during a World Cup event. Moreover, while the actual number may deviate slightly from the assumed

Assumption 6: Super Bowl LIX held in New Orleans generated a considerably low amount of landfill waste,
making it suitable to be an ideal reference when calculating the Landfill Index (LFI).
Justification: Super Bowl LIX took several measures to minimize landfill build-up. Indeed, it aims to divert ’at
least 90% of waste from landfills’[ 6].

• Carbon Emissions: Carbon emissions are arguably one of the major concerns when it comes to environ-
mental impact assessment. BioMed Central (BMC), a global authority in scientific publishing, defines the
metric carbon footprint reduction index (CaFRI) as ”a comprehensive greenness assessment tool prioritiz-
ing carbon footprint as the primary environmental impact”[ 9], highlighting the significance of measuring
carbon emission. Throughout our research, we roughly estimated the carbon emissions in New Orleans by
looking at carbon emissions per capita in Louisiana[ 4]. Since there are 65,719 offline audiences, the carbon
dioxide emission of Super Bowl LIX was about 21,660 tons[18].
• Energy Consumption: Energy consumption, especially electricity consumption, ”is at the heart of modern
economies” [ 8], meaning that energy consumption is also a key factor that influences the environmental
impact of Super Bowl LIX. Looking at energy consumption per capita in Louisiana[3], we roughly estimated
the total energy consumption to be 491,176,000,000 British thermal units (Btu)
• Water Footprint: Water consumption also plays a crucial role when it comes to assessing the environmental
impact of mega sporting events. ”Outdoor sports facilities use millions of gallons of water, putting pressure
on local water supplies, as well as using hazardous pesticides can pollute soil and contaminate run-off into
the water, threatening employees, players, and animals”[ 11]. Due to the scarcity of online data resources,
we first used AI to conduct the estimation (we will utilize a different way to estimate when constructing our
model). Considering drinking and restroom usage of the participants during Super Bowl LIX, we roughly
estimated the water consumption to be about 4 million litres.
• Air Quality: Air quality and pollution are considerably influential when assessing mega sporting events.
According to a study on Super Bowl, ”significantly higher levels of pollution are observed on Super Bowl
Sunday”[12][17] ”millions dying each year from exposure to and the side effects of air pollutants”. Based
on a specific study towards the AQI caused by NFL stadiums. It points out that ozone increases significantly
with more audiences. According to a study, the maximum AQI of an NFL event reaches 187, which is
considerably severe for the local environment[17].
• Landfill: The immense waste generated by mega-sporting events like the Super Bowl has a large impact
on the environment. Landfills not only cause havoc on natural ecosystems, leading to water contamination,
soil pollution, and air pollution, it also leads to the emission of methane, a greenhouse gas. By research,
around 2,000 tons of food waste is generated at Super Bowl parties. Even though the proportion of landfill
for Super Bowl LIX is just below 10%, this number is still considerably large for the amount of total trash
produced for the state[20].

7 National Impact Evaluation Model (NIEM)
Overview
The National Impact Evaluation Model (NIEM) evaluates the environmental impact of potential host coun-
tries for the World Cup, a single-sport championship tournament.
The World Cup is usually hosted by a single country, which is arguably similar to the Super Bowl. Unlike
the Super Bowl, which is a single-day event in a specific stadium, nevertheless, the World Cup is a month-long
tournament held in several different places within the country .
Therefore, to assess the environmental sustainability of potential host cities for the World Cup, we expanded the
City Impact Evaluation Model (CIEM) and developed National Impact Evaluation Model (NIEM), a specialized
evaluation model for the World Cup host countries.
Team # 16442 Page 18 of 27
When applying our model to potential host countries, we decided to pick 7 countries that have hosted the World
Cup before: Qatar, Russia, Brazil, The Republic of South Africa, Germany, South Korea, and Japan .
Notably, NIEM is essentially an extended CIEM. Specifically, we adjusted the 5 indices from CIEM and
added a new index. Afterwards, instead of utilizing the combination of Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and
Entropy Weight Method (EWM), we melded AHP with CRiteria Importance Through Intercriteria Corre-
lation (CRITIC) to better handle the textbfinterdependence between the indices while still ensuring objectivity
within subjectivity. Subsequently, we employed TOPSIS again to yield a comprehensive rating for each of the
potential host countries.

9 Strengths and Weaknesses
Strengths
• Instead of using singly an objective or a subjective weighting method, we strategically combined both
Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and Entropy Weight Method (EWM) when creating our City Im-
pact Evaluation Model (CIEM), which ensures fairness and reasonably reflects expert judgment, making the
results balanced and robust.
• Instead of adding EWM, we melded CRiteria Importance Through Intercriteria Correlation (CRITIC)
with AHP to adapt our National Impact Evaluation Model (NIEM), as the 6 indices in NIEM are more inter-
connected. This not only ensures objectivity within subjectivity but also better handles the interconnected
relationships between the 6 indices.
• Our evaluation models-CIEM and NIEM-encompass 5 and 6 key environmental impact indices , respec-
tively, each of which is further broken down into smaller contributing factors , making both evaluation
comprehensive and effective.
• Through sensitivity analysis, we observed that there is no significant fluctuation in our final results, proving
CIEM to be robust and reliable.
• Both CIEM and NIEM are designed for US cities and countries around the world, respectively. Thus, they
are arguably adaptable in most circumstances.
Weaknesses
• We applied certainstate-level data (e.g., energy consumption per capita) to the potential host cities, which is
a justified assumption. Nonetheless, this approach is only effective for approximations. Ideally, we should
procure city level data, which we didn’t found on the internet, by methods like on-site data collection to be
more precise and accurate.
• The units for certain variables in both CIEM and NIEM are non-standard, such as Btu and degree Fahren-
heit (imperial units), which may lead to misunderstanding and inconvenience when CIEM and NIEM are
used by others to conduct evaluations. To make both CIEM and NIEM more accessible, we should unite
every non-standard units into SI units or other standardized units .
• Both CIEM and NIEM assumes stable environmental policies and infrastructures, which is overly static
and realistically impossible, hindering their practical value.
