Commute Travel Behavior
Knowing where people walk or bike, and for what purposes, informs Glendale how to effectively
implement projects and programs that better serve residents. Glendale is a car-oriented
community. ACS data shows that in 2016, 2.1% of workers commuted to work daily by walking or
biking, while 2.7% used public transit. Although these statistics are important to the overall
discussion, they only include a portion of active transportation commuters and fail to measure non-
commute activities such as trips to stores, to schools, or for recreation.




         Homearea.com showed that Glendale has the highest commute times in the Phoenix
           metropolitan area, increasing by 6.4 percent from 26.5 in 2015 to 28.2 in 2018!
Trip Reduction Program
The Maricopa Regional Travel Reduction Program (TRP), which surveys employers and students to
determine commuting habits, provides a more accurate assessment of the total number of
employees/students traveling by alternative modes. According to the 2015 TRP survey, commuters
in Maricopa County walking and biking account for nearly 5 percent of all commuters that use
alternative modes. Of students that use alternative modes, over 8.6 percent walk, which is an
increase of 0.9 percent since the 2014 survey. Results of the TRP survey show employees commute
15.0 miles to work on average, while students travel an average of 7.1 miles to school. Overall,
27.6% of all TRP participants drive less than 5 miles to work/school. These 5-mile trips have the
potential to be converted to walking and biking trips through a connected, efficient active
transportation network.
