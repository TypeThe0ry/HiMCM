3.2. Classification system

  • International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED).
    • Non-finance education data up to reference year 2012 are based on ISCED 1997, as from the reference year 2013 ISCED 2011 is applied;

3.3. Coverage - sector

This data collection covers formal education programmes that represent at least the equivalent of one semester (or one-half of a school/academic year) of full-time study and are provided within the reporting country’s own territory. For further details see the UOE Manual on concepts, definitions and classifications.

Register-based data on students and graduates are available from 2007 onwards.

3.9. Base period

  • Data on students: Beginning of the school/academic year.
  • Data on graduates: School/academic year.
  • Data on personnel: Beginning of the school/academic year.
  • Data on entrants (schools): Beginning of the school year.
  • Data on entrants (academic programmes): Beginning of the winter term and the beginning of the summer terms. These data are summed up.
  • Expenditure data: Calendar year.

The UOE Data Collection consists of several datasets with different units of measures:

  • Enrolment (educ_uoe_enr): Number of persons
  • Entrants (educ_uoe_ent): Number of persons
  • Learning mobility and credit mobility (educ_uoe_mob): Number of persons
  • Education personnel (educ_uoe_per): Number of persons
  • Graduates (educ_uoe_grad): Number of persons
  • Language learning (educ_uoe_lang): Number of persons
  • Education expenditure (educ_uoe_fine): Euro

For a more exhaustive description of datasets and units of measures, see the UOE Manual on concepts, definitions and classifications.

The reference period for non-financial education data (such as students enrolled, entrants, personnel, regio, lang and credit mobility) shall refer to the school/academic year as defined nationally (year t/t+1). The transmission for enrolments, entrants, personnel, regio, and lang to the European Commission has to be done by 30 September in year t+2; for example 2024 data collection represents the academic year 2022/23. However, data for graduates refer either to all graduates during a calendar year (year t+1) or to the academic year as defined nationally (year t/t+1). The transmission for graduates and credit mobility to the European Commission has to be done by 30 November in year t+2.

For financial data the reference year is the fiscal year, e. g. data collection in year 2024 refers to the fiscal year of 2022. The number of students must be adapted to the fiscal year.

12.1. Relevance - User Needs

The UOE (UNESCO-OECD-Eurostat) data collection is highly relevant for Austria as it provides internationally comparable information on participation in education, graduates, educational personnel and education expenditure across all levels of the education system. By applying harmonised definitions and classifications, particularly ISCED, UOE statistics enable Austria to benchmark its education system against other countries, support evidence-based policy-making, monitor national and international education targets, and contribute to key international publications such as the OECD’s Education at a Glance. In addition, the UOE framework helps ensure the quality, consistency and international comparability of Austrian education statistics.

12.2. Relevance - User Satisfaction

There is no user satisfaction survey specifically targeting UOE users. However, Statistics Austria regularly conducts user satisfaction surveys and maintains ongoing communication with stakeholders. This continuous exchange helps identify users' needs and supports the further development and quality assurance of statistical products and services.

  • If Yes, how the length of their stay abroad is assessed? Incoming exchange students' length of stay in Austria is assessed through administrative data on the participation in exchange programmes. These data are reported each semester. Therefore, the shortest length of stay that is measurable amounts to one semester. Students with a stay of one or two semesters are excluded from the total population of students.
  • If NO, why they are not excluded?

Foreign languages learning

Thirdly, non-regular students (a temporary status of pupils, who do not understand the instruction language, which is valid at most for 12 months) are not covered.

15.3.2. Coherence - National Accounts

The extent to which statistics are reconcilable with National Accounts.

  • Calculation method:
    The UOE educational expenditure data are compiled on a cash accounting rather than an accrual accounting basis as recommended by the national accounts framework.
    In the UOE data collection all expenditure are assigned to the entity that makes the final expenditure, whereas national accounts asses the production value. The UOE data collection displays all expenditure in one single direction, whereas national accounts use accounts to look at output and intermediate consumption.
    In contrast to the national accounts the UOE data collection treats transfer payments as expenditure as well.

  • Scope of education:
    National accounts generally cover a much broader spectrum of education as they include training by the employment agency, adult education programmes, adult education centres, music schools, dancing schools etc., all of which are explicitly excluded from the UOE data collection.
    In the UOE data collection, ministries and administrative bodies should also be regarded as "education" and their expenditures counted, whereas in the national account such bodies are part of government administration.
    The systems to classify educational institutions vary substantially as the ISCED classification applied in UOE data collection is based on functional criteria, whereas national accounts assign all institutions to NACE and CPA levels.
    The criteria to classify entities as private and public are also different. The UOE data collection classifies entities based on who controlls it, whereas national accounts look at who is primarily responsible for the financing.This difference is particular apparent with the Universities of Applied Sciences which are classified as private institutions in the UOE data collection, while regarded as public by the national accounts.

18.3. Data collection

The questionnaires used can be found in CIRCABC Education and Training Statistics.

Data published by Eurostat is collected in 6 Excel files plus a quality report also in Excel format. The sheets are handed in via the EU portal EDAMIS. OECD and UNESCO-UIS collect additional countries and questionnaires that do not fall under EU legislation.

18.4. Data validation

As part of the compilation of the UOE data collections, numerous validation procedures are carried out, including the preparation of time series and the calculation of key indicators. These quality assurance measures help ensure the accuracy, consistency and plausibility of the reported data. In addition, OECD data validation software implemented in R is used and the built-in validation checks provided by Eurostat's EDAMIS system.
