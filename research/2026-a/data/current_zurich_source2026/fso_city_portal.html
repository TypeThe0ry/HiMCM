City Statistics – Quality of life in the cities

City Statistics provides comparable annual data on the quality of life in Switzerland’s largest cities and agglomerations. Here you will find indicators relating to demographics, housing, the labour market, education, health, mobility and other aspects, at various spatial levels. This provides local authorities, researchers and policymakers with a sound basis for comparing locations, analysing trends and implementing targeted measures.

      The topic in detail

      The City Statistics data collection (previously Urban Audit) provides information and reference measurements on various aspects of the living conditions in more than 900 European cities partner based on approximately 200 indicators covering three spatial levels: agglomerations, core cities and neighbourhoods.

      From 2009–2015 Switzerland conducted the project under the auspices of the Federal Statistical Office (FSO) and the Federal Office for Spatial Development (ARE), together with its most populated cities. Between 2016 and 2019, City Statistics was run as a joint effort between the Federal Statistical Office, the Federal Office for Spatial Development, the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) and the eight Swiss cities Basel, Bern, Geneva, Lausanne, Lucerne, Lugano, St Gallen and Zurich. Since 2020, the Federal Office for Housing (FOH) and the City of Winterthur have also participated in the project as partners. In 2024, the city of Biel/Bienne joined City Statistics.

      For the cities of Geneva and Basel, agglomeration communes in the neighbouring countries are also included, as the economic and social network extends beyond Swiss territory.

      Why do we collect these statistics?

      European cities face a variety of challenges, ranging from ageing populations to migration, urban sprawl and mitigating climate change. By contrast, Europe's dynamic cities attract investment, people and services, thereby stimulating creativity and innovation. Assessing the current situation is a prerequisite for any improvement, development and future monitoring.

      International comparison

      The cities and their agglomerations can also be compared at European level.

      City Statistics is an international project run by Eurostat, the European Union’s statistical office. Its aim is to provide information on European cities and their functional urban areas through around 200 indicators covering most aspects of living conditions, including demographics, housing, health, the labour market, education and the environment.

      Data collected in Switzerland within the scope of City Statistics are analysed at the level of cities and their agglomerations and transferred to Eurostat, enabling Switzerland to take part in this international project.

      Data from over 900 towns and cities taking part in City Statistics can be accessed from Eurostat’s database. Their availability varies by country and survey year as well as by subject matter. Provision of these statistics is purely voluntary as there is no legislation governing their collection. The graphics below give an idea of the comparisons possible.

        Details

      In Eurostat's City Statistics databases, data from the Population and Households Statistics (STATPOP), Buildings and Dwellings Statistics (BDS), the Structural Survey (SE) and Structural Business Statistics (STATENT) for a given reference year are those recorded on December 31 of the previous year (in line with Eurostat guidelines).

      For example: the permanent resident population recorded in Switzerland on December 31, 2023 is referenced to 1 January 1st, 2024 in the Eurostat database. There is thus a 1-year time lag between the Swiss and European reference years for the variables concerned.

      International perimeters

      To ensure that data from different countries are comparable and to establish a common framework for analysis at international level, Eurostat, the European Union’s statistical office, has created various territorial classifications.

      At European level, City Statistics uses the concepts of city and functional urban area. These are similar to the concept of agglomeration used by the Federal Statistical Office (FSO) for Switzerland's City Statistics, but Eurostat's definitions are based on different thresholds involving different perimeters. (Eurostat definition)

      The City (Eurostat definition)

      A city is a municipality in which the majority of the population lives in an urban core. Urban cores are determined on the basis a kilometre-scale grid of the population. These must have at least 50 000 inhabitants and a population density of more than 1500 inhabitants per km2.

      If an urban core spreads over several municipalities, all of the municipalities are taken into account. These are generally referred to as greater cities. In Switzerland, with the exception of St Gallen and Winterthur, the cities (or greater cities) extend over several municipalities according to the Eurostat definition.

      Functional urban areas

      Functional urban areas (FUA) are made up of a densely populated city and a less densely populated commuting zone, whose labour market is extremely well-integrated into the city (at least 15% of commuter flows towards the city).  If at least 15% of a city’s commuters travel to another city, the two are grouped within the same functional urban area. In Switzerland, this applies to Winterthur in relation to Zurich and to Thun in relation to Bern. Detailed information on the method and coding used for EU/EFTA states can be found in Eurostat’s Methodological Manual on City Statistics.

      Cross-border City Statistics

      City Statistics focuses on agglomerations spanning an international border, such as the Basel and Geneva areas. The analyses cover the entire urban area, both in Switzerland and abroad. Cooperation with regional and international statistical offices sheds light on similarities and differences. These spatial analyses make it possible to understand the dynamics and development of cities across national borders.

      In the scope of the project, data are not only collected at city level (core city level) but also at neighbourhood level and at agglomeration level. Consequently, statistics from adjacent French and German territories are important for the Geneva and Basel statistics. In collaboration with the FSO, the statistical offices of Geneva (OCSTAT) and Basel-Stadt have therefore decided to include the entire urban areas of Geneva and Basel, both within Switzerland and across national borders, in the statistics. With Eurostat's approval, Switzerland has thus undertaken cross-border data collection for City Statistics in collaboration with France's National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies (INSEE) and the German Statistical Office of Baden-Württemberg.

      Current cross-border perimeters around Basel and Geneva

      The perimeters of the cross-border agglomerations of Basel and Geneva used since 2016 for Swiss analyses correspond to the perimeters defined within the framework of the FSO’s new definitions of agglomerations (2012 status first, then 2020 status). 

      According to the FSO definition of agglomerations (2020, de, fr), the Geneva cross-border perimeter includes 216 municipalities (including 86 in Swiss territory and 130 in French territory; status of municipalities: 01.01.2024), and the Basel cross-border perimeter 207 municipalities (including 111 in Swiss territory, 72 in French territory and 24 in German territory; status of municipalities: 01.01.2024).

      For the operations carried out for Eurostat, the perimeters of the functional urban areas (FUA, de, fr) according to the Eurostat definition are used. 

      These perimeters offer a complete picture of the Geneva and Basel urban areas, thus facilitating comparison at national or international level.

      The allocation of Swiss municipalities (communes) to the various agglomerations and functional urban areas is available in the Swiss Municipalities application under Spatial divisions (de, fr, it).

      Atlas of Cities

      Discover maps of City Statistics partner cities and their agglomerations.

      Interactive Statistical Atlas of Cities

      The Statistical Atlas of Cities is a tool for the cartographic visualisation of key figures from Switzerland’s City Statistics with the country's participating cities. The maps can be retrieved at the level of different spatial units: agglomeration, core city and neighbourhoods on a national scale, as well as core city, national and cross-border agglomeration on a transnational scale. The atlas shows living conditions in the urban areas of the partner cities and allows comparisons between the municipalities and their agglomerations.

      Content and overview

      At the national and transnational levels, the atlas presents around 30 key figures in relation to various topics: demographic context, income and jobs, living conditions, environment, personal security, mobility, culture and leisure, economic context. Depending on data availability, the maps cover the spatial levels of agglomerations, core cities and neighbourhoods.

      Spatial divisions

      Agglomeration

      For the City Statistics project, the perimeters according to the FSO definition have been used as the agglomeration for the Swiss partner cities. In the Atlas of Cities, it is possible to view the agglomerations either in their entirety or by core city and peripheral communes.

      Core cities

      The core city is the core of the agglomeration. For City Statistics’ Swiss partner cities, this core corresponds to each city’s administrative commune (excluding adjacent communes within the agglomeration).

      Neighbourhoods and sub-city districts

      City Statistics uses the official FSO neighbourhood perimeters in the cities. To ensure that these perimeters fulfil the City Statistics requirements, for City Statistics cities these neighbourhoods have been aggregated at two higher levels (to reach a boundary of around 5000 inhabitants): urban sectors and urban districts. The first two units (neighbourhoods and urban sectors) can be seen in the atlas.

      Urban Switzerland: Cities and regions

      As part of the City Statistics project and in partnership with the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs SECO, the Federal Office for Spatial Development ARE and the Federal Office for Housing FOH, the FSO provides more than 180 variables in the form of interactive tables, broken down by the following spatial levels: agglomerations (centre, commuting zone and total), cities that fall under the statistical definition of a city, areas with urban character, urban-rural typology and labour market areas.

      The variables published in these interactive tables have been selected based on the list of variables collected by Eurostat as part of the City Statistics European project adapted and extended for Switzerland. The topics covered include demographics, housing, health, the labour market, education, environment, transport and tourism. The list of variables, their sources and specifications are described in the file below.

      The available spatial levels enable Switzerland's urban areas to be considered using various scales. The definitions and methods used to calculate statistics for these analytical regions and territorial typologies are described in the pages on spatial divisions.

      The interactive tables make it easy to select specific variables and territories as needed. A single table with pre-aggregated variables covering multiple topics and territorial classifications facilitates the use of the statistics and enables easier comparison across territories and dimensions. We nonetheless recommend that you consult the information in the list of variables and metadata before extracting data from the interactive tables.
