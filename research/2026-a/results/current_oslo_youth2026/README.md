# Resident districts distinguish supply, perceived provision and participation

Oslo's2026 published lower-secondary survey tables add direct resident-district context to the paper's existing supply indicators. Across all15 districts, reported sports-club participation in the preceding month ranges55–80%; perceived meeting-place provision rated very/fairly good ranges48–73%; parents sometimes/often lacking money for desired activities ranges8–21%. The corresponding spreads are25,25 and13 percentage points of the published rounded values. These are descriptive between-district contrasts, without item denominators, confidence intervals or an individual-level mechanism estimate.

`published_district_values.csv` contains180 cells:90 lower-stage descriptive cells and90 quarantined upper-stage cells. Both same-stage report copies agree on the selected all-district tables. The upper-stage report's local identity mismatch is documented in the [source notes](../../data/current_oslo_youth2026/README.md); no cross-stage or exact-age comparison is inferred. Districts refer to respondents' residences. These findings do not imply all students in a district have the same experience, or that facilities caused participation differences.

The contribution is a source-backed distinction between three questions: what young people think their neighbourhood offers, whether money constrains desired activities, and what activities they report doing. The aggregate data do not identify the intersection of these groups. An unweighted average of district percentages would not reproduce a citywide pupil-weighted statistic; no such average or fairness penalty is added to TOPSIS.
