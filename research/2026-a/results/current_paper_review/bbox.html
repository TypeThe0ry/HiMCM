City-speciﬁc shocks are illustrative, not forecasts. . . . . . . . . 26 Team # 18213 1 Page 3 of 27 Introduction and decision scope 1.1 What makes a city suitable for high-school life? A useful comparison connects a student’s school day with the opportunities and con- straints around it. Safety and air quality affect the daily setting; teaching resources and nearby higher education describe educational supply; housing, living costs, transport, travel, broadband and swimming facilities describe household pressures and everyday resources. Our operational deﬁnition of “best” is the conservative choice under these ten criteria, declared weights and retained evidence ranges. It provides a starting recommen- dation for the compared cities, rather than a claim about every student’s experience. The study uses the sixteen cities in the frozen team matrix: Amsterdam, Berlin, Dublin, Helsinki, London, Madrid, Milan, Oslo, Paris, Prague, Singapore, Stockholm, Tokyo, Vienna, Warsaw and Zurich. This is an inherited comparison set across sixteen countries, not a representative sample of all world cities. It exceeds the problem’s minimum of ten cities and spans different resource and cost settings. The ﬁndings concern this set; a broader claim would require an explicit sampling design and additional comparable observations. 1.2 Criterion selection and contribution The ten indicators preserve the agreed team framework and balance education with the conditions that make resources usable in daily life. Their directions and anchors appear in Table 1. Counts and city averages serve as scoped supply or setting measures. They do not directly measure friendships, student wellbeing, school quality, language ﬁt, dis- crimination, cultural participation or individual access. Those outcomes are relevant to a family’s choice; comparable student-level observations are needed to integrate them without inventing measurements or double-counting existing proxies. Our central contribution is a city decision that retains incomplete evidence. We improve source precision, compare a point-completion benchmark with an interval revision, and examine preferences and future conditions without changing the primary indicator set. TOPSIS provides one main quality score; AHP structures stated priorities, Monte Carlo measures speciﬁed perturbations, and VIKOR supplies a focused method comparison. The extensions test the recommendation rather than vote among an expanding set of ranking models. AI assistance supported research retrieval, calculations, code and prose preparation, in- cluding Exa source retrieval and extraction [19, 20].
