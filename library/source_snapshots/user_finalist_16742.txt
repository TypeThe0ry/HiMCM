Scoring Tomorrow’s Sustainability in Today’s Global Events
Summary
The Super Bowl is one of the most influential single-day sporting events in the
world.It attracts massive audiences, generate significant economic benefits, and bringing
host-city environments under an excessive amount of pressure. A significant amount
ofenergy use, transportation emissions, water consumption, andwaste generationall
contribute to substantial environmental impacts.In order to overcome these challenges,
we create an extensive ecological evaluation modelto determine which city would be
the most environmentally friendly host for the Super Bowl.
First, we developed a multidimensional indicator system that covers six key aspects
to measureenergy, noise, carbon emissions, water consumption, waste management,
andurban energy coefficient. We evaluated22 candidate cities—including 19 that have
historically hosted the Super Bowl and 3 that have never hosted the Super Bowl—
for indicators and calculated a standardized sustainability score for each indicator in Fig-
ure 2.Significant differences can be seen in the initial comparison, with some southern
cities receiving lower scores due to transportation and climate limitations, whileGlen-
dale has an overall indicator average about 0.16, andLas Vegas has an average around
0.24, which perform exceptionally well.
After that, we designed a hybrid weighted model thatintegrates the subjective infor-
mation from the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)andthe objective information from
the CRITIC method. In response to our results, among historical host cities,Pontiac with
a score of 0.77, followed by Indianapolis with a score of 0.76, and East Rutherford with
the score of 0.74. Meanwhile,Buffalo has the best out of the three non-host cities with
the score of 0.60. Thus, Pontiac is the best candidate to host the upcoming Super Bowl.
To validate the applicability of our evaluation framework to other major sporting
events, we extended it to Formula One (F1) racing by modifying existing indicators and
introducing two new indicators:Energy Recovery System efficiency and transportation
cost. By applying the expanded model to ten F1 candidate cities, we found thatMonaco
achieved the highest sustainability score of 0.854, which qualifies it as the most suitable
city for hosting F1 events among the candidates.
Following this study, we compared the environmental characteristics of the Super
Bowl with other major events and proposed two sustainability strategies:increasing
public transportation servicesandinstalling temporary noise barriers. We conducted
scenario simulations and sensitivity analyses to further evaluate the effectiveness of these
improvement initiatives and submitted a formal proposal to the Super Bowl organizing
committee.

1 Introduction
1.1 Problem Background
The Super Bowl, which has been organized since 1967, has evolved into one of the most
recognized single-day sporting events in the world. [1] Today, the Super Bowl has tran-
scended from just a championship game to become a cultural event worldwide. It at-
tracts over 100 million viewers each year and contributes billions of dollars in economic
impact to the host cities. [2-3] The presence of visitors, massive TV broadcasts, activities,
and business activities are contributing factors to its immense value to the economy.
However, behind the significant economic benefits, there are some serious environ-
mental challenges. Massive energy supplies are needed for stadium lighting, broadcast
technology, heating and cooling systems, and other operational requirements when host-
ing the Super Bowl. Millions of gallons of water are needed for field upkeep and turf
grass irrigation. Transportation-related activities, including lengthy flights, local com-
mutes, and logistics, also generate significant amounts of carbon emissions. The various
types of waste generated during the Super Bowl also create additional pressure on local
waste management systems. Therefore, the environmental impact of the Super Bowl is
usually greater than most other single-day sporting events.
The Super Bowl’s sustainability framework takes into account the host city’s geo-
graphic circumstances, energy structure, transportation effectiveness, climate traits, eco-
logical resilience, and water resource availability. Host cities must have excellent sus-
tainability credentials to reduce the environmental impact of hosting the Super Bowl, so
evaluating host cities from a comprehensive environmental perspective is the most criti-
cal factor in the Super Bowl’s selection of a host city.
2 Analysis of the Problem
2.1 Problem Restatement
To evaluate the environmental impact of hosting the Super Bowl and determine the most
sustainable host city, the following tasks must be completed:

cluding transportation emissions, noise pollution, water consumption, climate and
geographic conditions, energy structure, and waste-management performance.[4-11]
•Develop a comprehensive environmental evaluation modelby choosing relevant
indicators, standardizing data from multiple sources, allocating weights by using

that quantifies the sustainability of each potential host city.[12-15]
•Apply the modelto all cities in the U.S. that have hosted the Super Bowl and three
additional cities with NFL teams that have never hosted the event. Determine the
most environmentally friendly city to host the Super Bowl in 2029 by comparing the
outcomes of each candidate.[4]

2.2 Our Work
Our overall sustainability evaluation framework is illustrated in Figure 1.
Figure 1: Overall sustainability evaluation framework for Super Bowl host city selection.
We started by creating six key sustainability indicators to evaluate Super Bowl host
cities: energy, noise, carbon emissions, water consumption, waste management, and the
urban energy coefficient. Based on these indicators, we selected 19 historic host cities and
three potential future host cities that have never hosted a Super Bowl before.
Next, we collected the required data and calculated the values for each indicator for
each city. All values are normalized to produce complete and consistent indicator scores
for all 22 cities.
To determine the importance of each indicator, we applied both the Analytic Hierarchy
Process (AHP) and the Criteria Importance Through Inter-criteria Correlation (CRITIC)
model[14], where CRITIC derives objective information from data variability and inter-
indicator correlations, and AHP represents subjective expert behavior. After obtaining the
weights for each method, we gave each method a 50% weight to construct a balanced and
stable final weighting system. We then applied these weights to the normalized indicator
scores to calculate each city’s overall sustainability score.
According to our model, Pontiac ranks as the most environmentally sustainable host
city among all the other historic cities, while Buffalo ranks highest among the three cities
we chose.

Subsequently, we applied two sustainability strategies and obtained a score for each
city after implementing them, and compared the results to identify the differences. Ad-

Lastly, we extended our model to a different large-scale sporting event, the Formula

3 Model Assumption
To ensure consistency and fairness in our evaluation, the following basic assumptions are
made and properly justified below:
•Assumption 1: The number of audience remains stable across cities.
,→Justification:Super Bowl attendance consistently remains near maximum sta-
dium capacity (90%), meaning noise, waste, and transportation impacts are mainly
driven by city infrastructure rather than variation in attendance.
•Assumption 2: Transportation mode proportions reflect long-term mobility trends.
,→Justification:A single event does not significantly alter the share of car usage,
public transit, or air travel; therefore, transportation emissions can be evaluated
based on the structural mobility patterns of each city[7].
•Assumption 3: Climatic and geographic characteristics remain stable in evalua-
tion.
,→Justification:Environment-related indicators such as temperature, rainfall, hu-
midity, and urban heat islands change slowly over time, allowing stable compar-
isons in water consumption, ecological pressure, and energy demand.
•Assumption 4: Stadium energy consumption depends on structural characteris-
tics rather than event variation.
,→Justification:Broadcast, HVAC, and lighting follow standardized operational
requirements across host cities; thus, the primary environmental difference arises
from the local electricity mix and carbon intensity[6].
•Assumption 5: T urf-grass irrigation demand follows stable agronomic models.
,→Justification:Hydrological coefficients (Kc, effective rainfall ratio, irrigated area)
fluctuate minimally, so calculated irrigation demand reliably reflects local climate
rather than measurement uncertainty[11].
•Assumption 6: Waste-management systems reflect the city’s sustainable capacity.
,→Justification:Although the event temporarily increases waste production, waste
diversion and treatment efficiency are fundamentally constrained by the city’s in-
frastructure, making them reliable sustainability indicators[4].

9 Evaluate of the Mode
9.1 Strengths
Our model owns several obvious advantages, enabling it to be both effective and adap-
tive in evaluating major sports events. First of all, the indicators are comprehensive, in-
cluding six dimensions: energy, noise, carbon emission, water usage, waste, and urban
ecology coefficient. These dimensions reflect the key environmental factors jointly. Sec-
ondly, applying the mixed weighting method of AHP and CRITIC can ensure including
T eam # 16742 Page 23 of 26
both subjective experts’ opinions and objective statistical variability. The combine can
produce a rather balanced and credible indicator weight than using any other method
alone. Thirdly, the model is highly scalable. Through modifying and adding indicators,
the structure can be applied to totally different major events, like Formula One(F1), but
without losing the consistency of the methodology. Our application to the Formula One
events further proves the flexibility and robustness of the model under different circum-
stances, scale, and infrastructure needs. Lastly, the model supports real-world scenario
simulation. Governments can test different sustainable development strategies by ad-
justing specific indicators (like noise and public transport) and observing the impact on
the final scores immediately. This makes this structure a useful decision support tool for
urban planners and event organizers.
9.2 Limitations
Though the model owns so many advantages, there are still some limits that need to be
considered/ Firstly, some of the indicators rely on variables based on simplified or es-
timated values, but not directly measured variables, because of the limitations of data
availability. For example, the emission levels of transportation assume common travel
behaviors, and some of the indicators about architecture utilize normalized approxima-
tions. These simplifications may cause a little difference between the final score driven by
the model and the real situation. Secondly, the model evaluates the city only based on en-
vironmental sustainability, but does not directly include economic or social dimensions.
Although we intend to do this to clarify the scope, we may ignore some factors such as
economic feasibility, community engagement, and long-term impacts on urban develop-
ment. Thirdly, some of the events, especially the global events like F1, involve complex
logistics, like intercontinental freight, specialized equipment, and coordination of multi-
ple games. Though we have adjusted our indicators, the more specific data like detailed
energy recovery output, cargo weight distribution, and the exact water usage during the
repair cycle can improve the accuracy even more. Finally, though the process of weight
distribution is robust, a certain extent of subjectivity still exists. The AHP and CRITIC
method can balance subjectivity, but if the experts’ judgment or dataset characteristics
vary, the weight of some indicators still may change.
