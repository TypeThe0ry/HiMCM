2026 MidMCM 
Problem C: Redesign Education 
Note: Only teams with all members younger than 14 ½ years old may choose Problem C. 
 
 
Image Credit: Adobe Express 
 
Education has been a part of civilization since ancient times. The purpose of education has 
evolved over time and continues to evolve as the world changes. The purpose of education also 
varies from civilization to civilization, from country to country, and from culture to culture. In 
fact, education serves many purposes in modern societies. Education systems are designed to 
fulfill the needs of societies by achieving the purposes of education for that society. 
Acknowledging that current education systems all over the world might not be achieving the 
current needs of society as effectively as possible, the Committee on Making Awesome 
Pedagogy (COMAP) has asked your team to design a new educational system. 
 
Requirements 
1. Understand the Problem. 
• Purpose of Education: In your introduction, clearly lay out your philosophy about 
what you believe the purpose(s) of education should be. The purpose(s) you 
identify should guide your modeling process in the design of your educational 
system. 
• B ackground/Research : You may not be familiar with education systems other 
than the one you have experienced. Research a few other educational systems to 
identify different features of educational systems and how they achieve their 
purpose. What did you learn from your research that will be useful in constructing 
your model of a new educational system? What novel or unique ideas for 
education do you have that you would like to include in your educational system?   
 
2.  Create your model to design a new educational system.  
• Create a model for a new educational system that serves your purpose of 
education. Your model should be specific to middle school education. Your 
model should address multiple aspects of the design of educational systems 
including the content that will be addressed, how time will be allocated to 
learning, and the learning environment where education will take place.

• Content Model: Your model should identify educational content (e.g., topics, 
subjects, skills, etc.) that will be included in your educational system. The results 
of this part of the model should be presented in a 1-2-page curriculum guide.  
• T ime Model: You should construct a model of how educational time is spent in 
your system. How will time be allocated to your identified educational content? 
What will a typical school year/semester/month/week/day look like in your 
system? The results of this part of the model should be presented in a 1-2-page 
academic calendar. 
• P hysical Model : You should construct a model of what the physical learning 
environment of your educational system should look like. As you work on 
constructing this part of the model, you should consider the physical location(s) 
where learning will take place, the educational tools, resources, materials, and/or 
technology that will be important to your educational system. The results of this 
part of the model should be presented in a 1-2-page brochure for your learning 
environment. 
 
3. Apply your model. Create a 1-page advertisement or letter addressed to your fellow 
middle school students to convince them why they should want to participate in your 
educational system. 
 
4. Expand Your Model and Reflect on Your Analysis. Reflect on how your model for 
middle school education can be applied to the rest of the education system for students 
ages 5-18. 
 
5. Share Your Model and its Results. Write a 1-2-page letter to COMAP that explains the 
design of your educational system. Be sure to include how each part of your model is 
designed to achieve the purpose of education that you identified at the beginning of your 
paper.

may use up to 25 total pages for all your solution work and any additional information you want

We permit the careful use of AI tools, such as ChatGPT, although it is not necessary to create a

Guidance for MidMCM 
COMAP has a Judges’ Commentary article along with excerpts from top rated papers to serve as 
exemplars from the 2025 MidMCM contest available. T he commentary article provides guidance 
to both advisors and students. We also provide the following general guidance about MidMCM 
submission organization. 
Solutions must be in PDF format and submitted in one PDF document. This does not preclude 
MidMCM teams from doing mathematics, graphs, tables, sketches, etc. by hand and including 
pictures of their work in the single PDF document submission. As students move to high school 
and the HiMCM, we expect that submissions will be typed. For the MidMCM, advisors may 
technically assist students in putting their solution components into one PDF format file for 
submission. 
As with HiMCM, there is a 25-page limit for the submission document. This does not mean your 
solution must be 25 pages. A shorter submission is certainly acceptable. All portions of your 
submission (text, graphs, tables, charts, pictures, etc.) must be within one PDF document that is 
25 pages or less. We accept partial solutions. Then, if you have utilized any artificial intelligence 
tools, such as ChatGPT or translation software, you will append a Report on the Use of AI Tools 
after your solution – that report does not count towards the 25-page limit on the solution portion 
of your PDF. 
Optional Paper Layout In general, a complete solution submission may be organized as 
follows. Teams are welcome to adjust their submission format to meet the needs of their solution 
method and communication style.  
Summary Sheet – Write this summary after you have done all your work. This one-page 
summary is Page #1 of your solution document. It provides an overview of your work, including 
the problem, your approach, and actual results.  
Table of Contents – List the major items in your solution document to show the organization of 
your paper.

Introduction and Restatement of the Problem – Introduce the problem. Restate the problem 
and requirements in your own words. 
Assumptions with Justifications – State any assumptions you made to simplify and solve the 
problem and state why you made those assumptions.  
Presentation of Model and Solution – Ensure you address all requirements and describe what 
you are doing in solving the problem. Show and explain all your work, especially the model you 
created or selected and why. How it was applied. Use representations that help you tell the reader 
how you solved the problem (for example: equations, tables, graphs, pictures, etc.).  
Analysis of Your Work – Describe the implication of your work. Perform a sensitivity, which 
means to adjust or remove an assumption to see its impact on the result. Address any strengths 
(good points) and limitations (weaknesses) of your model and solution.  
Concluding Paragraph – End your solution paper with a final concluding paragraph that 
summarizes your results and/or makes recommendations for future work. 
Include all 1-2 page documents and memos, if not already included earlier.  
Reference List – List any sources that you used to solve the problem (for example, websites, AI 
tools, scientific, newspaper or magazine articles, etc.). 
Report on Use of AI Tools – This only applies if you used AI tools such as ChatGPT. These 
additional pages do not count toward the 25-page limit for the rest of your submission.
